When you're playing poker, a lot of the time you're not really playing for the money. The money really exists to keep the game in check, if there was no money people wouldn't take it seriously, there would be all-ins all the time, people wouldn't be as invested in other people's hands, really it would just be a different game. The money exists to show someone is bought into the idea of playing poker, and being surrounded by people bought in is a comforting experience, it's a shared investment into the activity, proof that we're there mentally (and monetarily).

Of course (sadly?) there are other activities than poker but I actually think buy-in happens all the same. When you "run point" on an activity you're that plans point guard. A point guard dribbles the ball up the court and facilitates the offense, ideally to score. If a point guard shows hustle during a fast break it encourages those around them to show that same energy and sprint down the court. Running point means you yourself have to buy in before anyone else, to lead is to care and commit before knowing anyone else's response.

Running point is pretty exhausting for that reason, you've committed before knowing what's going to happen. Maybe you polled a couple people for buy-in before or wondered how much commitment to the bit you should show. Ultimately your execution could go a variety of ways and you feel liable for how that goes for everyone you've warped into it. This can be even more frustrating if you've taken this burden in the friendship without feeling like it's been reciprocated. If someone fails to buy-in while you have your chips on the table it can feel like they're simply waiting for another table to open up and that can affect how you feel about yourself and others. I can be guilty of not buying in all the way too, I think we're all liable to it to some degree, but what are we doing it for? What is the point of having multiple tables of semi commitment over prioritizing a table of fully bought in players?

NFL hall of fame coach Bill Parcells has this quote "the best ability is availability" and it's always rang true to me in sports. There are certain players when they're healthy that can be so much better than others, but if they can't consistently play then the point is moot. With reliability and mutual buy-in comes chemistry, whereas vice versa this isn't true. Buy-in takes effort.

I've been re-evaluating my formula here, the people I care about running point for, the buy-in I have for certain relationships, the increasing amount of polarization I think I want to have with the people I hang out with. I think I need to go all in on people who are bought into me, and stop trying to create a game where I or the other person are just preserving a table that exists only as an ephemeral place. Being at a cold table isn't a reflection on you, but settling for it is.
